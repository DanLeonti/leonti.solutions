  • Position decides which style fits: tight from early seats and the blinds, balanced from the middle, loose only from the cutoff and button.
  • s = same suit (A♥ 5♥), o = different suits (A♥ 5♣). A5s is a play and A5o is a fold: suited adds a nut-flush draw, so you can still win when the ace is a trap.

Hand rankings

Strongest at the top. A hand from a higher row always beats any hand from a lower row, whatever the cards: a pair of twos beats ace-high, a 6-high straight beats three aces, a 7-high flush beats an ace-high straight.

You make your best five cards from seven: your two hole cards plus the five on the board, in any combination. You can use both, one, or none of your own cards.

Outs and the rule of 4 and 2

An out is any unseen card that turns your hand into the winner. Count them, then multiply: by 4 on the flop (two cards still to come), by 2 on the turn (one card to come). That gives your chance to hit within a point or two of the exact number. Hover a row to see an example hand and board.

Counting outs. Flush draw: 13 cards of your suit, you see 4 (two in hand, two on board), so 9 remain. Open-ended straight draw (you hold 7-8 on a 5-6-K board): any 4 or any 9 completes it, 8 cards. Gutshot (7-8 on 5-9-K): only a 6, 4 cards.
Don't double count. If one card completes both your flush and your straight, it's still one out. A flush draw plus an open-ender is 15 outs, not 17, because two of the straight cards are also your suit.
Using it. Compare your chance to hit against the price. Facing a half-pot bet you need to win 25% of the time: a flush draw (35%) calls, a gutshot (17%) folds. Facing a pot-sized bet you need 33%: now only the big combo draws call.
